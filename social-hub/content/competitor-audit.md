# Competitor audit and OkayChat.in creative direction

**Reviewed:** 15 November 2026. This is a public-content and positioning audit, not a measurement of engagement or a full historical post scrape. Findings describe publicly indexed company pages, blogs, and case-study libraries; formats/hook patterns are editorial observations, not performance claims.

## Recurring hooks and formats

Across the public material reviewed, recurring editorial approaches include:

1. **Outcome-first case-study headlines:** quantified improvements or scale claims attract attention, but they require attributable evidence and should never be generalized to OkayChat.
2. **Problem → workflow → result:** a team’s communication pain is made concrete, then the platform is presented as part of a process.
3. **Industry-specific use cases:** order questions, appointments, lead qualification, delivery updates, and service requests make the channel relatable.
4. **Feature/product walkthroughs:** shared inbox, automation, integrations, templates, and dashboards are shown as practical tasks.
5. **Educational/API content:** explainers, guides, and technical documentation help developers and buyers understand setup and constraints.
6. **Lifecycle framing:** acquisition, conversion, support, and retention organize use cases across the customer journey.

## Gaps to own

- **Less “growth at any cost,” more operational clarity:** show a small, useful next step for a real team without implying guaranteed revenue, speed, or response outcomes.
- **Cross-industry by design:** rotate examples across education, clinics, real estate, local services, ecommerce, SaaS, agencies, and general support; do not let one vertical define the brand.
- **Team workflow as the center:** explain what happens to a conversation next—owner, status, context, follow-up—rather than only emphasizing broadcasts.
- **Honest build-in-public:** say “we’re building” and show concepts as concepts. Make limitations and feature status legible.
- **Developer + operator bridge:** translate API mechanics into the team workflow they support; address both audiences in the same weekly mix.
- **Consent-aware messaging education:** emphasize relevant, expected communication, opt-in, approved templates and applicable platform rules.

## Brand-specific creative direction

**Voice:** clear, warm, practical, calmly confident, inclusive. Prefer short sentences, ordinary words and concrete examples. Avoid hype, jargon without explanation, competitor callouts, and promises about outcomes.

**Core editorial lanes:**

- Conversation operations: ownership, labels/status, notes, handoffs, follow-ups.
- Industry-in-context scenarios: one scenario per asset, with a broad-audience framing.
- Developer education: API concepts, webhooks, template constraints, integration questions—only when verified and accurately caveated.
- Product/build notes: demonstrable current state or transparently marked concept/roadmap.
- Responsible messaging: relevance, permission, opt-out expectations, and template policy.
- Team habits: practical checklists and support playbooks.

**Visual system:** use only the six supplied colors and Inter. Suggested accessible combinations: charcoal on white/pale green; white on primary green or dark green; charcoal on light gray. Keep body text at readable sizes and maintain strong contrast. Use generous whitespace, simple grid/cards, numbered steps, and abstract message/inbox shapes. Any mock interface must be labeled “Illustrative concept” if it is not a verified current screen. Never use WhatsApp’s logo/trade dress in a way that implies endorsement. No stock colors, skin tones, shadows, photo backgrounds, or decorative accents outside the palette.

**Channel roles:** LinkedIn for explainers, workflow carousels, and build notes; Instagram/Facebook for concise carousels and short vertical video; Threads/X for one insight plus a question; YouTube Shorts for quick walkthroughs. Adapt length and aspect ratio, not the underlying claim.
